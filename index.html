About the data

Sources & more

A hospitalised injury is a person admitted to hospital after a road crash and discharged alive. The unit is the person, not the crash. People treated only in emergency departments are excluded under some states' rules, and deaths are excluded. Data: BITRE Hospitalised Injury (September 2023), from the AIHW National Hospital Morbidity Database.

Q1 · National trend

Hospitalisations rose through the 2010s, dipped in 2020, then recovered

34,033 people were hospitalised after road crashes in 2011 and 39,505 in 2021. Two states changed who they count during this period (marked on the chart), so levels across those lines aren't directly comparable. The data alone can't tell us why 2020 was lower.

About the data

Where the numbers come from, and what they can't tell us

Source

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE), Hospitalised Injury, national workbook (September 2023), raw sheet filtered to Cause of injury = "Traffic". Data from the AIHW National Hospital Morbidity Database. Accessed [date].

What is counted

  • Counted: people admitted to hospital after a road crash and discharged alive.
  • Not crashes: the unit is the injured person.
  • Not all injuries: emergency-only cases are excluded under some states' rules.
  • Not deaths: in-hospital deaths are excluded.

Things to keep in mind

  • Reporting breaks: Victoria (2012) and NSW (2017) changed who is counted.
  • Counts, not rates: no population data are used.
  • Unknown values are kept as their own category so totals add up.
  • No causes: the charts show patterns, not why they happened.

How we built it

BITRE workbook → KNIME (filter, check, aggregate) → CSV files → D3.js charts.
